New in 0.5.2, build 86 (2026-09-22)
Learn the game's sounds no longer plays menu sounds of its own. Moving through the list and pressing Enter each played a menu sound, which ran straight into the sound you were trying to learn; now the only thing you hear on that screen is the sound itself.
Replaced every menu sound that was a copy of one of the game's older menu sounds, and added a set for groups. Moving between controls and pressing buttons now use Call of Duty 4's interface sounds, typing and deleting its terminal-text sounds, voice listening its screen-in and screen-out; errors, reaching an edge and wrapping round come from Injustice 2's menus; and the ten group sounds come from Injustice 2's online and menu sounds, Call of Duty's interface and Battlefield 2042's. Every source is recorded in tools/audio_import_table.tsv. The sound pack has been rebuilt with all of them.
Chat messages sent to you while you are in the lobby now arrive. The lobby read the network with its own handler, which knew nothing about chat, so messages in your language channel, private messages and group messages sent while you were there were dropped without a trace.
Group chat has been redesigned. Groups are now kept on the server between sessions, and you can be in up to five of them. Joining takes an invitation you accept or decline: the owner used to be able to add anyone without asking. The owner and moderators invite and remove people; the owner can also appoint moderators, hand the group to someone else, rename it or disband it; and anyone can leave. Alt+Shift+Slash opens your groups -- each with how many members are online -- your waiting invitations and Create a group, and each group opens to its members, their roles and who is online. Alt+Slash messages your quick-chat group, the only group you are in, or one you pick. Group messages, members joining and leaving, invitations, declined invitations, being removed, promotions and renames, and groups being created or disbanded each have their own sound, which you can hear in Learn the game's sounds, and every change goes into a groups buffer. Groups also have touch gestures.
The item catalogue is data, like weapons. The two hundred or so items that are not weapons -- ammunition containers, armour, patches, explosives, medication, team kits, tools, electronics, vehicle supplies and maintenance parts -- each have a file in content/items with their name, category, description and any mechanics, which the client and the server both read at startup. They used to be written into the game's code one by one, with their categories re-guessed from their ids as they were registered. content/items/README.txt describes the format, and release builds now include the folder.
Using a Carrier-Breaker Guided Missile no longer also straps it to your chest as a plate carrier. The server decided whether an item was armour, which body slot it covered, how much it protected, how long it lasted, what it weighed and which patches fitted it, all by looking for words in the item's name: anything containing "carrier" or "vest" was torso armour, and anything containing "heavy" was the heaviest armour there is. The bomb vest was being equipped as torso armour for the same reason. Armour, patches and launched munitions now say exactly what they are in their data files, and a patch goes on the most worn piece of armour you are wearing that it actually fits. Armour and patch help now gives the real numbers and the list of armour each patch repairs.
What's new reads each release as its version, then how many changes it has, then its build number and date ("0.5.2, 45 changes, build 86"), so you hear what matters first and can move on before the build number. In Learn the game's sounds, moving through the list is quiet again and Enter plays the sound you are on, so you can look for a particular sound without hearing every one on the way.
The buffer manager key works whatever you bind it to. The game checks plain actions by their key alone, so that a few of them can have Shift or Alt variants, and that meant an earlier action on the same key took the press first: with the buffer manager on its default Alt+Shift+B, the zone key (B) saw a B and used it up. An action now leaves a key alone when the modifiers you are holding match another action's binding on that key exactly, which fixes the same problem for any chord you bind that shares a key with a simpler action.
Touch and keyboard now play the same game. Touch had its own copies of the movement and action code, and they had drifted a long way: a touch drag walked at a flat pace with no momentum, and ignored the weight you carry and your weapon's handling, so a heavily loaded player moved faster by touch than by keyboard; there was no way to sprint, climb, steer a vehicle or hold the trigger down for automatic fire; and about two dozen keyboard actions had no gesture at all. Gestures now press and hold the very same actions the keys do and go through the same code. New gestures: drag with two fingers to sprint; drag one finger up or down in the top band to climb; drag left or right in a vehicle to steer; tap and hold with two fingers to keep the trigger down; and defaults for nearby players, health, coordinates, zone, help, the inventory menu, ammunition, stamina, pills, the buffer manager, group chat, Options, volume, lifts and ship transitions, the horn, the player list, the message of the day, ping, uptime, push-to-talk mode and choosing who to track. All of them are listed, and can be rebound, under Gesture bindings.
Fixed the Armoury dropping your connection a few seconds after you opened it. To find out which preview sounds existed it loaded every one of them from the sound pack, which on a weapon with dozens of recordings held the game up for seconds at a time; the lobby then came back, saw it had not heard from the server in all that time, and declared the connection lost before reading the messages that were waiting for it. The Armoury now checks the pack's index instead, a pause is no longer mistaken for a silent server, and screens opened from the lobby read the network with the lobby's handler instead of the match's.
Fixed a failed connection attempt leaving your next connection unstable until you restarted the game. When the first attempt failed -- because your network dropped out or the server was down -- its connection request stayed alive in the background, and went through as soon as the server was reachable again, alongside your new one. You then had two connections to the server at once: some messages arrived twice, others went to the connection that had never logged in, and when the server or the network gave up on the spare one the game treated it as losing the server. The failed attempt is now torn down completely before the next one starts, every message goes to the connection you logged in on, a stray second connection is closed on sight, and only the loss of your real connection is reported as a disconnect.
Page Up and Page Down set the menu music volume in steps of five percent in every menu, the main menu included, and the setting is kept between sessions. The old list menus had these keys but forgot the level on exit, and the main menu lost them altogether when it became a list. On a slider or number field, Page Up and Page Down still move that control by a large step.
Text fields, number fields and lists have their missing sounds. Typing a character, deleting, cutting, copying, pasting, selecting everything and running into either end of the text each have their own short sound; text fields had none at all. Number fields tick with each step and sound the edge at their limits. In lists, ticking and clearing an item in a multiple-choice list, selecting all, and typing a letter nothing starts with now sound too, and opening and closing a branch of a tree has its own pair of sounds. The new recordings come from the Battlefield 2042, Halo 5 and Battlefield 4 interface libraries, and are levelled to match the rest of the set.
Menu sounds play again. The new menu cues lived only in the sounds folder, and a sounds.dat built before they existed plays silence for a sound it does not contain, so most menus went quiet. Every cue now falls back to the older menu sound it replaced when the pack lacks it, and the pack has been rebuilt with the whole set. A regression test now checks every menu and awareness sound is inside sounds.dat.
Fixed Left and Right not changing tabs in Options or any other tabbed screen. The check added in this release to play a sound at the first and last tab read the arrow keys before the code that changes tabs, and reading a key uses it up, so the tab strip never moved. Each key is now read once.
Equipping a second holster puts it straight into the free slot. It used to ask you to choose between both slots and then refuse the one that was already full.
Opening a screen during a match no longer freezes the match behind it. About twenty screens -- choosing a holster, a weapon's details, reviewing a message, the buffer manager, the speech settings and the map builder among them -- never serviced the connection while they were open, so other players stopped moving, gunfire went unheard and the server's messages piled up until you closed them. Every screen now keeps the match running.
Feedback and bug reports work the same way as support tickets now: one list per screen, Back returns to where you were, and a report or piece of feedback is a list to arrow through with its replies or the developers' comment. Both used to reopen their menus from scratch every time you backed out of something. A bug report's status reads as plain words ("not yet looked at" rather than "unflagged"), the report form asks what you did, what happened and what you expected, and staff choose a status from a list and add their comment in two short steps.
Rebuilt the support ticket screens. A ticket is now a list to arrow through -- subject, who opened it and its status, why it was closed, the description, then each reply -- with Reply and Close after it, instead of a column of read-only edit boxes. Every screen goes back to the one it came from: they used to reopen each other from scratch, so the Back button on a ticket opened a new support menu on top of everything before it. Closing your own ticket now asks first, and the ticket list says how many tickets it holds. Line breaks typed on Windows no longer leave stray characters in tickets and replies.
Tracking follows the same rules as the motion tracker for anyone not on your team. A tracked enemy gives off a beacon, and answers the track information key, only while the tracker would show them -- close by, moving or firing -- and goes quiet when they keep still out of range. Tracking used to hand you any player's exact coordinates anywhere on the map, which no amount of hiding could beat. Teammates can still be tracked anywhere, all the time.
When the server takes more than five seconds to answer, the game now says why it is asking whether to keep waiting; the explanation used to sit in a box behind the button you were on, where you only found it by tabbing back.
The nearby players key is now a motion tracker, so keeping still is worth something. Anyone within eight tiles still shows up whatever they are doing. Further out, a player shows only while they give themselves away: moving in the last second and a half, out to thirty tiles, or firing in the last three seconds, out to sixty -- thirty if their weapon is suppressed, since a suppressed shot carries no further than footsteps. Someone standing still and holding fire beyond eight tiles does not show at all. Teammates always show, out to sixty tiles. Every contact says whether it is moving, firing or still. It used to name everybody within thirty tiles whatever they were doing, which made an ambush impossible to set and pointless to look for.
The server now turns away clients older than 0.5.2 and tells them to update. It accepted anything from version 0.12 onwards, and an older client would now roll its own vehicle damage and healing against a server that no longer listens to either. The rewards and controls help files also describe the rewards, tracker and guidance as they now work.
The team shop says how many points the pool has, marks anything the pool cannot yet afford and by how much, and asks before spending: the points belong to the whole team, and one press of Enter used to spend them.
The Team screen is one list: your team and points, then every member by name and role, then messaging, inviting, the team shop and leaving. Enter on a member offers what you are allowed to do to them -- remove them, make them a manager, or change their permissions -- and inviting picks from the players who are online and not already on your team. It was four tabs with a button or two in each, and every action made you type a player's name, and for permissions the permission's name as well. Leaving the team and removing a member now ask first.
Equipment: press Enter on a slot to take off what is in it, confirm, and the list updates in place and stays on that slot. Removing something used to open another Equipment screen on top of the old one every time, and taking off armour closed the screen instead.
What's new shows the changelog one release at a time: a list of releases, newest first, each saying how many changes it has, and Enter opens that release with one change per line to arrow through. It was the entire changelog, well over a thousand lines, in a single text box.
Learn the game's sounds is one list, and moving onto a sound plays it; Enter plays it again. It was a category list, a sound list and a Play button that had to be tabbed to for every single sound. It now also covers the tracker tones for a player above, below and level with you, the guidance beacon and arrival sound, the killstreak reward, team chat, and every menu sound.
The Manual entry on the main menu opens the manual. It looked for readme.txt and readme.md, and the game ships readme.html, so all it ever did was say the file was missing. On Windows it now opens in your web browser, where the headings and tables work properly with a screen reader; anywhere else it is shown in the game as plain text. The manual itself now says it is for 0.5.2.
Health potions no longer make you nearly unkillable. After drinking one, your own game healed you 500 every fifteen seconds for two minutes -- four times your whole health bar -- and the ultra potion 1500 every twenty-five seconds for five minutes, and the server accepted any amount of healing a client asked for, up to 2000 at a time and as often as it liked. It also started even when the server had refused the potion because it was still on cooldown. The healing over time is now the server's: a health potion goes on healing 100 over twenty seconds after its immediate effect, the ultra potion 300 over a minute, and the Residual Healing effect you can check shows only when the server has actually granted it. A health potion also now correctly says your health is already full, which it never could before.
The server decides who you are and where you start when you deploy. It used to take the name, map and starting position from your client's spawn request, so a modified client could enter a match under another player's name, or start anywhere it liked. It now uses the name you logged in with and the spawn point it chose itself, and ignores a spawn request it did not deploy. The "has entered" announcement also waits until the map has been checked, instead of announcing a map that is then refused.
The manual has a new section on finding people and places, covering the clock-face bearings, the points of interest guide and the tracker beacon, and its description of Check for updates matches what the updater now does.
The game menu you open with Escape during a match is now one list, like the main menu and the lobby, and Escape closes it again. It has gained Options, so you can change volumes and settings without leaving the match, and Help topics. Status is a list you arrow through rather than a read-only text box. "Exit game", which actually took you back to the lobby, is now called Leave the match and asks exactly that. Equipment, Team and the staff panel used to open a new game menu on top of the old one each time you came back from them.
Check for updates can actually update the game. It needed Git for Windows installed to download anything, and even with Git its last step -- copying the new files over the old ones -- never ran: it handed its paths to PowerShell in a way PowerShell treats as more commands rather than as data, so the game closed, nothing was replaced, and the download was left in a folder beside the game. It now downloads GitHub's zip of the release with PowerShell, which every copy of Windows has, unpacks it, replaces the files once the game has closed and starts it again. If the download or unpacking fails you are told and nothing is touched.
Removed two leftovers: a second copy of the help browser that could never be reached, and the counters from the old speed-hack check.
Points of interest can guide you. The list is nearest first and gives each place's distance, clock-face bearing and height from you ("Saint Ewald's cathedral, 40 tiles at 2 o'clock, 12 above"); press Enter on one and a beacon sounds from that place, more often the closer you get, until you arrive and are told so. Open the list again to stop, or pick somewhere else. It was a list of names and distances that did nothing when you chose one.
The speed-hack check no longer throws out players with a bad connection. It fired after five late server heartbeats in a row, which a few seconds of unreliable Wi-Fi produces just as well as a cheat, and then closed the game without a word while the server announced to everyone that you had launched Cheat Engine. It now counts thirty heartbeats against the time they took: lag delays heartbeats but does not lose them, so only a game clock that really is running fast -- by half as much again, for two windows in a row -- is caught. If it is, you are told why before the game closes, and everyone else is told plainly that your clock was running too fast.
Your account and Killstreak rewards are now lists you arrow through, not stacks of read-only edit boxes that screen readers announced as editable and that had to be tabbed through one field at a time. Account shows your name, whether the account is registered and its email, and your credits; it no longer throws away chat and private messages that arrive while it waits for the server to report your credits. The rewards list now says what the server actually does: each reward comes once per life, so dying and redeploying starts the count again and being revived does not, and every kill earns 10 to 20 credits that also count for your team.
Fixed every death stacking a new lobby and a new match on top of the one that just ended. Returning to the lobby after dying, being sent back from a match or the match ending all opened a fresh lobby from inside the old match, and deploying from it started the next match inside that, so a long evening carried every match you had played. The lobby now exists once, like the main menu: going back to it from anywhere unwinds straight to it.
Distant sounds -- far gunfire, explosions, aircraft -- start from the right direction. They were handed your facing in degrees where the sound system wants radians, so each one started panned somewhere arbitrary and jumped to the right place a moment later.
Tracking a player gives you a beacon. While you track someone, a beep sounds from where they are every couple of seconds -- the high tone when they are above you, the low one below, the plain one on your level -- so you can walk to them by ear rather than pressing a key for coordinates. The server had been sending a position for this beacon all along and the game ignored it. You are told when your target goes down, leaves for another map, drops off the tracker or leaves the game, and when the beacon comes back. Cancelling the tracking menu no longer stops tracking the player you were already following.
The nearby players key tells you about everyone nearby. It only ever named the last player it found within range, so with three people round the corner you heard about one. It also named players who were invisible, which the tracker had always refused to do. Nearby players, nearby items and tracking now all describe things the same way: nearest first, the straight-line distance in tiles rather than two sides of a triangle added together and called feet, a clock-face bearing ("14 tiles at 2 o'clock") in place of phrases like "slightly in front and a fair distance off to the right", and how far above or below you it is, not just whether. Teammates are marked as your team. Ammunition on the ground is named by its weapon instead of by its internal code, and a long list ends by saying how many more there are further out.
Vehicles now have one health, the server's. Every client used to roll its own random damage for each explosion and bullet it heard near a vehicle, decide by itself when the vehicle was destroyed, throw its own passengers out, and tell a server that had no handler for the message. The same vehicle could be wrecked on your screen and untouched on everyone else's. Bullets, grenades and mortar shells also each used a different armour formula, and only bullets ever announced that a vehicle had been destroyed. Now all of them go through one server function -- armour takes the hit, a quarter of every hit reaches the hull anyway, and whatever the armour cannot absorb reaches it in full -- and every client hears the same result. Crashing into a wall is reported to the server, which decides the damage itself and will not take it more than once every six tenths of a second. People inside a vehicle that is destroyed are now actually hurt by it, which the game had always said would happen and never did.
Rebuilt the lobby the same way as the main menu: one list, with the four maps at the top because deploying is what the lobby is for, each with a line on what the map now is, then players online, your account, killstreak rewards, the armoury and leaving. Choosing a map used to mean opening Modes, which offered a single mode, which opened a list of maps described as they were before this release -- Ghost Town still promised a saloon and a railway depot. Escape goes to Leave, F1 still lists who is online, and the lobby reopens on the last thing you chose. The in-game map guides, the maps and modes help, the turbolift help and the manual's map table are rewritten to match the new maps.
Fixed the game stacking a new main menu on top of the old one every time it returned to it. About thirty places -- leaving Options, reading the changelog, a failed connection, leaving the lobby, a dropped match -- went back by calling the main menu again from wherever they were, so every visit added a layer that never unwound, and a long session carried all of them. The first main menu is now the only one: asking for it from anywhere returns straight to it and discards whatever was in between.
Rebuilt the main menu as one list: arrows move through it, Home and End go to the ends, typing a letter jumps to that entry, Enter opens and Escape goes straight to Exit. It was five tabs of buttons with the exit filed under Settings, and it handled the arrow keys itself on top of the dialog handling them too. It reopens on whatever you last chose, and its music now actually plays -- it was loaded and never started -- and keeps playing through options and the changelog instead of restarting from the beginning every time you came back. The column-of-buttons layout is still available under Accessibility.
The map ambience volume now controls map ambience. Ambience beds were playing on the gameplay channel, so the slider labelled for them did nothing to them and a firefight could crowd them out; they now have their own pool on the world channel with an earshot suited to a sound you walk through. Text prompts that keep the match running while you type now keep every positional sound following you, instead of leaving ambience and distant fire frozen where you were when you started typing.
Sliders work the right way up. Up and Right raise the value and Down and Left lower it, as every other slider anywhere does; they were reversed, a leftover from sliders being used to pick maps from a list, which made every volume control in the game work upside down. Touch swipes follow the same rule.
Reorganised Options into six categories named for what you are looking for -- Gameplay, Sound, Controls, Touch, Accessibility and Online -- from eleven tabs, several of which held a single checkbox, and one of which put voice commands and voice chat together because both had "voice" in the name. Nothing was removed. Every option is reworded to say what it does rather than what it is called internally. Volumes and mouse sensitivities are sliders instead of boxes you type numbers into, and the volume sliders take effect as you move them with a sound on the channel you are adjusting, so you set a level by hearing it; Cancel puts them back. Opening the key binding editor, the gesture list or saved servers from Options no longer throws away changes you had made but not yet saved, and turning the low-vision display on offers full screen once rather than forcing it back on every time Options is saved.
Every control in every dialog now has its own sound. There used to be two -- a tick for moving and a click for pressing -- so ticking a checkbox, reaching the end of a list, pushing a slider past its limit, switching tabs and closing a dialog were all either the same tick or silence. Now: moving between controls, moving through the items of a list, hitting the end of one, wrapping round to the first control, pressing a button, ticking and clearing a checkbox, a slider step, a switch changing, a tab changing, a dialog opening and closing, and an error are all different, all trimmed short enough never to be in the way, and all matched in level; the old menu sounds ranged over eighteen decibels and the wrap cue was nearly inaudible. The older list menus use the same set. They play on the interface channel, so the Interface volume now actually controls menu sounds: they were going out on the gameplay channel. A pistol's safety catch, which played a menu click, now sounds like a safety catch.
Voice commands now work in menus for the things that make sense there -- next and previous buffer, repeat message, cancel -- and say that match commands need a match instead of acting on a player who is not there. Added "where am I". Listening has its own start and stop sounds in place of the mode cues, and the confirmation that used to play a nine-second recording is a short chime. The Windows mixer mute no longer "restores" the game to minus one percent: Windows reports that until the game has played its first sound.
The low-vision display finally shows something. It drew all its text with no font loaded, which on this renderer draws nothing, so it was a dark screen with a couple of lines on it. It was also laid out against the window's unscaled size and ran off the edge on any display set above 100 percent. It now shows, in high-contrast text as large as the window allows: where you are across the top, the last thing the game said in a bordered panel, the control with focus and what kind of control it is, and during a match the zone you are in with your coordinates and facing along the bottom. Anything too long for a line is shrunk and then wrapped rather than cut off.
Desktop notifications now come from Infinite Warfare. They were being sent under the application ID "170505" while the Start menu shortcut that makes an ID legitimate declared a different one, because the helper passed a number where the plugin wants text and gave the shortcut call its two arguments the wrong way round; it also left a Start menu entry literally named 170505, which is now removed. A toast is only raised while the game is in the background -- with the window focused, the buffer sound and the spoken line have already done the job -- and it now covers your name being said in chat, as well as private messages, ticket replies and server problems. The test in Options explains what to check in Windows if the toast does not appear.
Fixed every one of the NVGT helpers, each for its own reason. None of them was serviced anywhere but the gameplay loop, so in menus, the lobby and every dialog -- most of the time the game is open -- the tray icon ignored clicks, voice commands were never heard and the low-vision display never redrew. They now run from the form layer that every screen goes through, and from the older list menus, as well as during a match.
Filled all hundred and eight of them, plus ten more for weapons that had a fire sound and nothing else. Thirty-seven weapons now have the click that ends their reload, eighteen have the sound of the magazine coming out, eighteen have a round going into a body, and thirty-five fire selectors now say which position they just landed on -- by the number of detents, so semi-automatic is one click, burst is two and full automatic is three and you can tell them apart without being told. The M72 rocket launcher's entire sound set existed and had never once played, because it was imported with an underscore the game's naming convention does not have. The DR Resonance Projector had a fire sound and nothing else. Every import goes through one tool against a written table: destination, source, what was done to it and why, loudness-normalized to the same target as everything else, with the table shipped beside the audio. Insurgency: Sandstorm handling foley and flesh impacts, and Fortnite energy weapons for the things that do not fire bullets.
Replaced the sound audit. The old one looked for string literals, which misses practically everything this game plays: a weapon's filenames are built at runtime out of its sound profile, a surface's out of its recording family, a service's out of its type. A weapon with no fire sound is not a missing literal, it is a weapon that goes click. The new audit derives the contract from the weapon definitions themselves -- fire, draw, holster, empty, reload, the end of the reload, unloading, three impacts, and one selector cue per fire mode when a weapon has more than one -- and checks the surfaces and ambience every map asks for. It found a hundred and eight actions with no sound at all.
Fixed eight surfaces that had been silent since they were imported. Their footstep variants were numbered six to thirteen instead of one to eight, and the client counts upward from one and gives up after two misses, so steel grating, deep water, long grass, loose scree, matting, packed grass, bridge decking and thick weeds all played nothing at all. Renumbered, and the checker now refuses any surface family whose variants do not start at one.
Replaced the map checker. It now rasterizes the emitted file in declaration order and floods it the way a player actually moves -- one-unit steps, climbable columns, and lifts -- which is the only way to catch a doorway sealed by a wall written after the room, a stair with a two-unit riser in it, a spawn inside a building, an island nobody can walk to, or a patch of floor with no zone on it so standing there says nothing. Rectangle-union coverage passes all of those. It also checks that every material has a surface line, that every ambience bed, object sound and service type exists in the pack, and that every loot item is a registered one. Between them the builder and the checker turned up forty-one genuine defects in the four maps while they were being written, including a lighthouse buried under its own gallery, a wharf gantry that walled its island in half, an aqueduct with no way through it, a transit platform a single unit short of its stair, and a Jedi Temple whose fountain hall punched up through the terrace above it and took the staircase with it.
Rebuilt Coruscant as a vertical slice of the Galactic City, because the only honest way to build Coruscant is downwards. Six stacked levels, each a solid slab with the next one resting on it: Level 1313 at the bottom with the Crimson Corridor and the sump market and a clinic nobody upstairs knows about, the Works above it with a dead foundry and the pumping gallery, CoCo Town with Dex's Diner and the Collective Commerce machine shops, the Uscru Entertainment District with the Outlander Club and the Galaxies Opera House facing away from each other one street apart, and the Senate District on top with the Rotunda, the Republic Executive Building, and Monument Plaza with Umate standing in the middle of it. The Jedi Temple is on its own four-setback ziggurat across the skylane, with the Room of a Thousand Fountains and the processional hall on the top and five spires round them, and a ladder up the tallest one to the highest place a player can stand on this map. Three turbolifts serve every level from the same position on each, which is how a building works and how a player learns a map. Two vent shafts of maintenance rungs run from 1313 up through the Works, and they are the reason the undercity is not sealed. Above everything are three skylane gantries and the interchange that joins them, reached by rungs from the esplanade.
Rebuilt Freya's Ascent as a vertical city, which means the map is now actually about height. Six terraces are cut into Freya's Tears, each set back from the one below so what is left in front of it is the lower terrace's shelf, and every one of them has its own trade: the landing field and the bar at the bottom, cut-rock housing and the catchment basin at Lowreach, the ropeworks and the foundry at the Stitchworks, market and hostel and infirmary at Middlehold, the mill the waterfall drives at the Cataract, administration and the observatory at Highreach, and the tether anchor on the Crown. There are exactly four ways up and they cost different things: switchback stairs that zigzag so no single flight holds the whole climb, a goods lift with five stops that is fast and is the obvious place to be met, the adits that connect that lift to each shelf -- the lowest is a hundred and forty units of tunnel through solid rock with nothing in it and nowhere to turn off -- and maintenance rungs straight up the cliff face, slow and completely exposed. The Tears themselves land on a different shelf at each level, always to your east and always a little quieter than they were, which makes them the one thing on the map that tells you how high you are without your asking. Above all of it, reached only by the tether climber from the Crown, is the Folkvangr Spindle: a ring of corridor with four spokes to a hub and four compartments off it, and nothing outside the ring at all.
Rebuilt Shattersea as nine islands and twelve bridges. Landmarks, services, loot and spawns are no longer placed by eye. The builder now rasterizes the finished map exactly as the game reads it, floods it from a spawn point, and settles every one of them on a square that is both standable and genuinely connected to the rest of the world. The old maps had landmarks inside the counters they described; this one refused to build until a vending machine came out of a two-by-four pocket behind a shop counter that was walled in by a bookcase. Loot is scattered as separate single-square spawns through a room rather than heaped on one tile.
Replaced the map builder. The old one had five primitives and a rule that a room is a box; the new one knows what a street is. A street now writes its carriageway, both pavements and a kerb zone on each side, named by the stretch you are standing on rather than end to end, so "Meridian Avenue between Fourth Street and Market Street, west pavement" is a thing the game can tell you and stepping off a kerb is a thing you hear. A junction is its own place with a zone per crossing. A room writes its floor, its walls, its roof, an interior zone and an acoustic space in one call, because a room without acoustics sounds like open ground and that gets forgotten on the fortieth room of a map. A doorway writes the hole, the portal and the zone, all three or none. A stair is a stack of one-unit treads. A bridge has a deck, rails and piers instead of being a solid block of rock you cannot walk under.
Gave both sound pools an earshot limit. They had none, which meant every looping sound a map declares stayed decoded for the whole match no matter where you were, and every gunshot anywhere on the map was mixed even when it was four hundred metres away and inaudible. Out-of-earshot loops are now unloaded and reloaded when you come back to them, and a distant one-shot is simply not played. A ranged source measures from the edge of its own rectangle rather than its corner, so standing inside a large ambience bed always counts as being on top of it. This is what makes it affordable for the new maps to carry real ambience at all.
Made climbing a thing the map declares instead of an accident of how tiles happen to stack. Any surface whose name contains ladder, rungs, netting, scaffold or climb is now a climb: you walk into its column, it tells you so once, and the up and down keys take you along it at a rung per press with the right sound. The automatic step you take over a one-unit rise or drop no longer applies to those surfaces, so a ladder is something you use rather than something you drift up sideways. Pressing up or down where there is nothing to climb now says so -- including "the top of the climb, step off to one side", which is the one thing you actually need to hear up a ladder -- instead of doing nothing at all, and trying to climb into a solid surface thumps against it like walking into it does.
The coordinates key now answers with something worth pressing it for: your position, the surface under your feet, how far it is down to the next solid thing beneath you if you are above one, and the zone you are standing in. It used to read out three numbers.
Split what a surface is called from what it sounds like. A tile's material used to be a sound filename, which is why the maps said "concrete5" and "grass3" and why they between them used about ten of the hundred and ten recorded surfaces in the pack: nobody writes "wetconcrete" into a map knowing a player will hear the word. Maps now name surfaces in English and declare the recording family once per material on a surface line, so "cracked concrete", "steel grating", "wet cobbles" and "shingle" are all sayable and all sound like themselves. Footsteps, landings, falls and wall impacts on both the local player and everyone else resolve through that table; a material with no line falls through about fifty substring guesses, and anything still unmatched becomes concrete rather than silence.
Gave the server the same tile index. Authoritative projectile physics asks "what surface is under this point" through a bilinear sample of four map cells across eleven candidate heights, and the slope gradient repeats that four more times, so a single bullet step was around a hundred and seventy full scans of every tile rectangle on the map. On a city-sized map that is millions of comparisons per shot per tick. The server now answers each of those from one grid cell.
Replaced the map's rectangle lookups with a real spatial index. Asking what tile, zone, acoustic space or doorway you are standing in used to compare your position against every rectangle on the map and then remember the answer in a dictionary that was never pruned, so a long session on a large map slowly filled memory with one entry per cubic metre ever visited. Those four layers now sit on a uniform grid that answers the same question from a handful of comparisons inside one cell, with no cache at all. Later records still win, so a kerb painted over a road is still what you are standing on. Sound occlusion benefits most: tracing a sound from its source to your ears used to rebuild a candidate list by scanning every space, portal and tile on the map for each ray, several times a second.

The version a person reads and the version the handshake sends are now two separate things: the connect_me packet still carries a bare number because the server parses it with string_to_number, but every window title, crash log, stats screen and manual now prints "0.5.2" instead of "0.512".

Android touch gestures
Added full touchscreen control for Android. When the game runs on a mobile device it now reads touch gestures and turns them into game actions, so the whole game can be played without a keyboard. The screen is split into three horizontal bands: the top band handles inventory and status, the middle band handles movement and combat, and the bottom band handles the message buffers and the game menu. Gestures are recognised by which band your finger starts in and how many fingers you use: for example a one finger swipe left or right in the top band cycles your inventory, a two finger tap in the middle band fires your weapon, and a two finger double tap in the bottom band opens the game menu. Driving a vehicle unlocks extra middle-band gestures such as a three finger swipe to change seats and a one finger drag up or down to accelerate. A full reference is available from a new "view Android touch gestures" entry in the main menu and from the options menu; you can also reach it from the in-game menu on a phone.
Because Android does not support the desktop audio form windows, the menus and dialogs those windows provided are replaced on Android with touch-navigable menus and the native on-screen keyboard, so account entry, the game menu, equipment, the player and tracking lists, the weapon panel, help entries, and points of interest all work on a phone. Menus can be driven by swiping up or down to move, double tapping to choose, and a two finger tap to go back. Nothing changes on desktop. An option, "simulate Android touch mode", is available in the options menu for testing the touch interface on a computer.

New in 0.18
Weapons now use a realistic magazine and clip-based ammo system.
Each weapon has a specific feed device with a real-world capacity and must be reloaded manually.
Press R to reload. The current feed device is ejected and a fresh one is loaded from reserve.
Press Shift+R to unload the current magazine back into your reserve pool.
Press A to hear rounds loaded, ammo type, feed device name, and rounds in reserve.
When a weapon runs dry, you hear the empty click and a reminder to press R.
Firing is blocked while a reload is in progress.
Beta: all players start with a full weapon and generous reserve magazines.
