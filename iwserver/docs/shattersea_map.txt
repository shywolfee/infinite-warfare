Shattersea

Nine islands in a sea on a world with two moons and four tides a day, joined by twelve bridges. Five hundred and sixty units across.

Kelphaven is the fishing harbour: drying lofts you can walk under and climb onto, the fish auction and the lifeboat station. Cinder Rock is a volcanic plug that steps up in three shelves to the lighthouse, its gallery and the tide siren. Saltmere is four evaporation pans, each ankle deep and one step above the last, and the saltworks. Spindlewharf is the cargo island: container lanes, a transit shed and a crane gantry twelve above the road. Guildhall Isle is the middle of everything, where eight bridges meet: the great hall, the market on the square, the harbour office and a bell stage up a ladder. Glasswick has a sand quarry, three furnace halls and a catwalk over them. Roostcliff has a cliff path, a funicular to the top, and five terraces along the cliff edge. Thornwater has two cisterns, a pumping station and an aqueduct with a walk along its top and an arch through it. The Drown is the old city: one step below every bridge, under water because of it, with three ruins still roofed and the best equipment on the map inside them.

The sea is six below every quay. You can go in and you can swim, and both are loud; the sea is named in sixteen sectors so you still know where you are. Getting out means finding a slipway or a ladder, which is what makes the bridges the route and the water the gamble.
